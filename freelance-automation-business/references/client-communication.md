# Client Communication

Every message is a draft for the human to send. Head each draft with a one-line context tag:
`To: <client> | Re: <project> | Situation: <classification> | Goal: <what this message achieves>`.

General register: friendly-professional, short paragraphs, one ask per message, always end with
the next concrete step and who owns it. On marketplaces, keep all communication and payment
on-platform — moving off is a ToS violation and kills dispute protection.

## Situations and how to play them

### New lead responds to a proposal
Reply fast (flag as top priority in Review & Send). Answer their question, restate scope in one
sentence, propose the concrete start: "If that works, accept the offer I'll send for $X and
I'll have the first output to you by <date>."

### Kickoff (contract accepted)
Confirm in writing: deliverables, exclusions, delivery date, revision policy, what you need
from them (files, URLs, access). This message is the scope anchor for everything later.

### Progress update
Send one mid-project update even when nothing is wrong ("halfway done, on track for Friday,
here's a sample of the output"). Silence is what makes clients nervous, and nervous clients
leave 4-star reviews for 5-star work.

### Revision request — in scope
Just do it, cheerfully, once. "Good catch — updated version attached." The included revision
round is reputation insurance; spend it gladly.

### Scope change / out-of-scope request
Never a flat no; never a silent yes. The pattern: **affirm → anchor → quote**.
> "Happy to add the email alerts. The current $140 covers the scraper fix we scoped; the alert
> system is about 2 hours more, so I'd add it as a $60 milestone. Want me to include it?"

### Client goes quiet with work delivered
Day 3: friendly nudge with the deliverable re-attached. Day 7: "I'll mark this complete on
<date> unless you'd like changes — the revision round stays available this week." Then use the
platform's completion mechanism.

### Unhappy client / dispute risk
De-escalate first, be right second. Acknowledge specifically, state what you'll do and by when,
and over-deliver on the fix. Only stand firm (politely, citing the written scope) when the ask
is clearly new work and the client won't pay — and flag any brewing dispute to the human as
`[DECIDE]`, since a refund is sometimes cheaper than a 1-star review.

### Retainer conversion (the profit center)
Trigger on: 2nd+ job from the same client, or a deliverable that will need upkeep (scrapers
break when sites change; monthly reports recur). Pitch after a successful delivery:
> "Sites like this change every few months and the scraper will need small updates when they
> do. If it's useful, I offer a maintenance retainer — $75/month covers monitoring, fixes
> within 48 hours, and small tweaks. Cheaper than re-hiring for each break, and it stays on
> the platform."

### Asking for a review
Once per completed job, after they've confirmed it works: "Glad it's working! If you have 30
seconds, a review helps a small freelancer like me a lot." Never ask twice; never hint at what
rating to give.
