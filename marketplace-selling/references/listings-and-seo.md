# Listings and SEO

A listing has one job: get found by the right buyer and convert them. Those are two separate
problems — **search** (title, tags, category) gets the listing seen; **conversion** (photos,
price, description, reviews) turns the view into a sale. A listing can be perfect at one and
fail at the other, so diagnose which is broken before rewriting anything.

## Search vs. conversion

The single most useful diagnostic is the ratio of **views to sales**.

- **Few views** → a search problem. The title, tags, or category aren't matching what buyers
  type. Fix keywords.
- **Plenty of views, few sales** → a conversion problem. Photos, price, description, or trust
  (reviews, shipping time) are losing the buyer who already found you. Fix the first photo and
  the price before anything else.

Chasing the wrong one is the common waste — rewriting tags on a listing that gets plenty of
views and no sales does nothing.

## Titles

The title is the heaviest SEO lever on Etsy and Amazon. Write for the search engine first,
then make it readable.

- **Front-load the most important keywords.** Both Etsy and Amazon weight the first words most,
  and mobile truncates the title. The buyer's search term should be near the front.
- **Write how buyers search, not how you think of the product.** "Personalised wooden cutting
  board" beats "Handcrafted heirloom charcuterie masterpiece" — search the plain words people
  type. Use the platform's own search-suggest (start typing a term and read the autocomplete)
  as free keyword research.
- **Include the concrete attributes** buyers filter and search on: material, size, colour,
  occasion, recipient. "Variegated Monstera Albo plantlet, rooted node, rare houseplant" says
  what it is and catches multiple searches.
- **Etsy** allows ~140 characters — use them, comma-separated keyword phrases are fine.
- **Amazon** wants a structured title (Brand + product + key features + size/qty) and penalises
  keyword stuffing and promotional language ("SALE", "best").

**Example — a printed planter, Etsy:**
`Geometric Hexagon Planter, 3D Printed Succulent Pot with Drainage, Modern Desk Plant Pot, Boho Home Decor Gift`

That single title reaches searches for hexagon planter, succulent pot, desk plant pot, boho
decor, and plant gift — five entry points instead of one.

## Tags and keywords

- **Etsy: use all 13 tags.** Each is up to 20 characters and multi-word phrases beat single
  words ("succulent planter" not "planter" + "succulent" separately, because buyers search
  phrases). Don't waste tags repeating a word already carrying weight in the title, and don't
  duplicate near-identical tags — spread across the different ways people might search.
- **Cover synonyms and adjacent intent:** the object ("moss terrarium"), the use ("desk decor"),
  the occasion ("birthday gift for her"), the style ("cottagecore"), the recipient ("plant lover
  gift").
- **Amazon: back-end search terms** — fill the hidden keyword fields with synonyms, spelling
  variants, and terms you couldn't fit in the title. No commas needed; don't repeat title words
  (wasted space); no competitor brand names (a policy trip).
- **Long-tail beats head terms for a small shop.** You will not outrank established sellers for
  "planter", but you can rank and convert for "hexagon desk planter for succulents" — fewer
  searchers, but they want exactly what you have.

## Photos

Photos do most of the conversion work, and the **first photo does most of that** — it's what
appears in search results and decides the click. Invest here before anywhere else.

- **First image:** the product clean, well-lit, filling the frame, on a simple background. On
  Amazon the main image *must* be on pure white; on Etsy a styled but uncluttered shot wins the
  click.
- **Then show:** scale (in a hand, on a desk, next to a common object), detail/texture,
  the product in a real setting, any personalisation, and packaging for live/fragile goods (it
  reassures buyers the plant/terrarium arrives safely).
- **Live plants:** photograph the *actual* plant or a representative one and say which — buyers
  of rare plants are wary of bait-and-switch, and "you'll receive a similar plant" honestly
  stated prevents disputes.
- **Consistency:** a coherent lighting/background style across a shop reads as professional and
  lifts the whole catalogue.
- Use all available slots; Etsy allows 10, and video noticeably lifts conversion.

Good photos are the highest-ROI improvement for almost every underperforming listing, and the
one sellers most often skimp on.

## Descriptions

- **First two lines carry the weight** — they show before "read more" and, on Etsy, feed search.
  Lead with what it is and why to want it, not a greeting.
- **Answer the buyer's real questions:** exact dimensions and weight, materials, what's included,
  care/finish, processing and shipping time, personalisation options and how to send them,
  live-arrival/breakage policy. Every question the description doesn't answer is either a
  message (friction) or a lost sale.
- **Set expectations honestly** to pre-empt returns: layer lines on prints, natural variation in
  wood and plants, that a live plant differs slightly from the photo. Under-promising here lowers
  returns and bad reviews, which protects search rank and account health.
- **Structure for skimming** — short paragraphs or bullets, key facts scannable.
- Keep a **reusable shop boilerplate** (shipping, policies, care) appended below the
  item-specific copy, so every listing is complete without rewriting it.

## Channel differences

| | Etsy | Amazon | Facebook |
|---|---|---|---|
| Title | ~140 chars, keyword phrases | Structured, no promo words | Short, plain, benefit-led |
| Keywords | 13 tags + title | Back-end search terms + title | Hashtags help discovery |
| Photo main | Styled, uncluttered | Pure white background required | Clear, honest, show flaws for used |
| Description | SEO + story + specs | Bullets of features + specs | Short, price, condition, pickup/ship |
| Ranking driven by | Relevance × conversion × recency | Sales velocity, relevance, reviews | Recency, price, engagement |

## Diagnosing a listing that isn't selling

Work the ratio, then the funnel:

1. **Is it getting views?** No → search problem: rework the title's first words and the tags to
   match real search language; check the category and attributes are set.
2. **Views but no sales?** → conversion problem, fix in this order: the **first photo**, then the
   **price** against comparable listings (run the margin script — you may have room to drop, or
   you may be underpricing and reading as low-quality), then the **description's** completeness,
   then **trust** (reviews, processing time).
3. **Sells but you're not making money?** → not a listing problem; a pricing/fee problem. See
   [pricing-and-fees.md](pricing-and-fees.md).
4. **Was selling, then stopped?** → check for new competition, a price change, a slipped metric,
   seasonality, or a search-algorithm shift. Compare against what changed, as with any
   diagnosis.

The discipline is the same as everywhere in this skill: identify which stage is actually broken
before spending effort, rather than rewriting the whole listing on a hunch.
